# Audio authenticity and perception

Bodh asks when a clip's documented production history diverges from how authentic it sounds to listeners. A corpus label describes how the clip was produced. A listener response records a judgment under a particular task and stimulus context. Perceived trustworthiness is a first impression. The truth of a statement requires independent evidence.

## Priority datasets

- **Behavioral Signals Deepfake Detection Demo** is the local audio pipeline check. Its 22 labeled clips let us demonstrate ingestion, feature extraction, and checks for recording differences. The publisher supplies provenance labels and no listener ratings. [Dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/behavioralsignals/deepfake-detection-demo)
- **Lavan et al. (2025)** links voice provenance to listener judgments. The local Experiment 1b analysis uses ratings of realness and perceived trustworthiness for human recordings and voice clones. Each rating matches one stimulus, and the human and cloned versions share a source-speaker key. The public Experiment 2 folder was empty when checked. Its three-way comparison remains a published reference. [Paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0332692) · [OSF project](https://osf.io/mrw5q/)

## First results

### How listeners perceive voices

Bodh matched 7,998 ratings from 50 listeners to 80 Lavan WAV files and verified the originals against the OSF file hashes. The 40 human recordings averaged **57.6 in realness** and **57.1 in perceived trustworthiness** on the 0–100 scales. The 40 corresponding clones averaged **56.6** and **58.9**, respectively. These are descriptive means with equal stimulus weights. The authors' mixed-model confidence intervals for both contrasts include zero. Across 40 speaker pairs, 9 differed by at least 10 realness points in either direction. The English stimuli share source speakers across conditions and use different spoken excerpts. [Author paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0332692)

The Belin reanalysis uses 9,000 public ratings from 500 listeners. Each listener rated nine morphed “hello” stimuli in the experimental continuum and nine in the control continuum. Within the experimental continuum, the paired S7 minus S3 difference was **+67.4 points** on the 0–500 scale (95% CI +57.6 to +77.3). The control contrast was −7.6 points (95% CI −14.7 to −0.4, unadjusted). These intervals describe sampling variation across listeners conditional on the fixed stimuli. The two continua used different source voices and the morph varied multiple acoustic properties. [Author paper and supplements](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0185651)

The Belin paper describes z-scoring each listener across all 18 stimuli. Standardizing each listener within each nine-stimulus block yields the published rounded experimental S3 and S7 means (−0.39, +0.40) and reproduces the reported paired t statistic to rounding. Standardizing across all 18 yields −0.66 and +0.18. Both implementations preserve the direction of the experimental contrast. The supplementary audio is supplied as two complete montages, and the stimulus-wise analysis uses the published ratings matrix. [Author methods and results](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0185651)

In the published Experiment 2, 50 participants heard 120 stimuli. Clones were called human 70% of the time, human recordings 72%, and generic synthetic voices 39%. The estimated clone-minus-human difference in realness ratings was −0.84 points (95% CI −4.86 to +3.18). The fixed English material, curated recordings, single synthesis provider and recruited listeners limit generalization. [Lavan et al., Experiment 2](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0332692)

### What can confound an authenticity detector

Behavioral Signals' public demo contains one bona fide and one spoofed clip in each of 11 languages. All files were 16 kHz mono PCM. Median duration was **6.19 s for genuine clips and 3.14 s for spoofs**. The median within-language quiet-frame gap fell from **21.3 to 3.7 percentage points** after excluding quiet edges. The sample contains different utterances and two spoof generators. Recording and editing differences can explain part of the observed gap. [Behavioral Signals dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/behavioralsignals/deepfake-detection-demo)

The [evaluation report](evaluation.md) describes reused VCTK speakers and recordings that the official speaker ID check alone missed, along with the resulting model comparisons. Müller et al. documented substantial cross-domain degradation when detectors were evaluated on web-collected speech. [Müller et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16263)

## Computational budget

The reference system was an Apple M3 Pro MacBook Pro with an 11-core CPU and 18 GB of unified memory. The local research runs used ARM64 Python 3.12.2, NumPy 1.26.4, SciPy 1.13.1, Matplotlib 3.8.4 and FFmpeg 9.0.2.

The 22-clip speech pilot took **1.80 seconds** including report and plot generation, with about **177 MiB peak Python RSS**. That memory figure excludes the FFmpeg child processes. The run used serial clip processing and one OpenBLAS thread.

Caching 40-band float32 frames at 100 frames/s takes about 55 MiB per audio hour. Float32 mono 16 kHz waveforms take about 220 MiB per hour. The 600-clip ASVspoof sample occupies about 39 MiB. The spectrogram Transformer has 575,488 encoder parameters, and the 20-epoch CPU pretraining run took about 19 seconds on the reference system.

## Evaluation design

- **Provenance.** Freeze source hashes, versions, licenses, grouping keys and official splits before development. Count missing groups and metadata. Match source recordings and detect overlap beyond exact file hashes, including re-encodes and clips with shared parents. Keep generator families and channels separate when the evaluation question calls for it.
- **Detection.** Later, lock thresholds on development data, report EER, ROC-AUC and error rates at application-relevant operating points. If reporting precision or calibration, state the assumed class prevalence and sample selection. Report per-generator and per-condition counts, clustered uncertainty by source or speaker, and a genuine cross-corpus holdout. Use an ASV-specific cost metric only for the corresponding challenge protocol.
- **Perception.** Preserve the independent endpoint, presentation arm, stimulus pairing, and listener ID. Prefer crossed listener/stimulus effects or appropriately clustered uncertainty over treating thousands of repeated ratings as independent. Report binary discrimination and response bias separately from perceived realness or trustworthiness. Reserve held-out stimuli and listeners for any later predictive evaluation. Test causal acoustic effects with a randomized design, matched text and appropriately controlled manipulations.
- **Shared representations.** The 16 kHz log-mel view is a compact baseline. Preserve native originals so later studies can quantify effects of bandwidth, codecs, recording gain or preprocessing. Use a separate feature set for prosody once pitch and timing measures are validated on representative speech. Keep asset metadata for future frame-level and cross-modal analyses.

All experiments ran locally. The Transformer used random initialization and weights learned in the documented Bodh runs. The source publishers provided the speech and rating datasets. See the [reproduction guide](reproduction.md) for acquisition and analysis commands.
